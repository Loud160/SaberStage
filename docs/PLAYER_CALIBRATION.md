# Player calibration architecture

## Shared-Quest player profiles

SaberStage provides five fixed local profile slots for people who share one headset or want separate configurations. The Avatar tab exposes them through one `Player Profile` dropdown (`Profile 1` through `Profile 5`). A profile contains only that player's calibration and the complete Avatar settings group, including selected avatar, avatar quality/visibility controls, controller-to-wrist offsets, display-clone placements, and per-avatar retargeting choices. Camera, recording, preview, broadcast, and every other SaberStage subsystem remain shared and are not changed when the active profile changes.

The migrated `Default` player becomes `Profile 1` and continues to use `PlayerCalibration.json`, so an existing installation does not require recalibration. Profiles 2 through 5 store calibration beneath `PlayerProfiles/<profile-id>/PlayerCalibration.json`. The settings document snapshots each profile's Avatar settings independently and restores the active Avatar UI when switching profiles.

Version 3 calibration derives player arm span and confidence from the accepted T-pose endpoints. Compatible version-2 files are refitted from their saved captures on load. The resulting span drives avatar uniform scale when credible; a bounded legacy height-scale fallback remains available for incomplete or low-confidence calibration.

Player calibration is separate from VRM geometry measurement, tracking acquisition, UI, and per-frame solver state. `PlayerCalibrationSession` owns the guided capture workflow and source trajectories. `PlayerCalibrationProfile` owns versioned persistent player measurements and derived fit parameters. `RuntimePlayerProfile` is the fixed-size, trivially copyable gameplay view. The solver receives both the avatar's authoritative skeletal calibration and the player's runtime profile; switching VRM files does not rewrite or invalidate player measurements.

The profile is stored beside SaberStage's other mod data as `PlayerCalibration.json`. Version 1 stores profile and algorithm versions, UTC timestamp, tracking configuration, Basic/Advanced mode, accepted static summaries, full accepted motion trajectories, confidence values, and derived grip, reach, lean, step, crouch, and turn parameters. Loading discards incompatible, malformed, or incomplete source data and keeps the generic solver active. Compatible files are refitted from their source captures instead of trusting stale derived values. Saving uses a temporary file and backup rename so a partial write cannot become the active profile.

## Guided capture

Basic calibration contains neutral, arms down, T, forward, Y, chest, left/right lean, left/right step, squat, forward duck, and left/right 45-degree body turn. The natural ready pose means standing comfortably upright and facing forward, feet in an ordinary comfortable stance, with both controllers held naturally in front of the waist or lower chest and the elbows relaxed. It is not an arms-down pose and it is not a T pose. The following arms-down pose explicitly asks for both arms to hang relaxed at the player's sides. Advanced calibration adds outward-45, overhead, same-side shoulder, cross-body, four head-only orientations, forward/back lean, and forward/back step. Basic takes about 97 seconds of fixed preparation and capture windows before retries; Advanced takes about 182 seconds.

Opening Basic or Advanced calibration first presents an introduction; it does not start a timer or alter the active profile. `Start Automatic` preserves the streamlined sequence and advances directly from each accepted capture to the next countdown. `Start Step-by-Step` waits at an explicit `Start Step` gate before every countdown, then waits at `Continue` after each accepted capture before revealing the next instruction. Tracking updates cannot advance either user gate. Both styles use the same capture plan, validation, fit, review, and saved profile, and Restart retains the selected progression style.

Each started step has a five-second preparation countdown with one tick per second. The timing rule depends on the kind of step and is stated explicitly on the panel:

- **Pose steps:** move into the requested pose during the countdown. Be in the final pose before `MEASURING` and the measurement tone begin, then hold still until the shutter.
- **Movement steps:** remain in the natural ready pose for the complete countdown. Do not start moving until `MEASURING` and the measurement tone begin, then follow the instruction until the shutter.

An audible 440 Hz measurement tone marks the exact sampling window, and a synthetic two-click shutter cue marks completion. Both use a persistent 2D source that is allowed to play while Unity's menu listener is paused. Static captures record one second; motion captures record 2.6 seconds at a capped 45 Hz. The capture layer keeps HMD, raw controller, observed saber/grip, velocity, angular velocity, and timestamp data. Static fitting filters unstable frames, uses coordinate medians, and uses hemisphere-correct normalized quaternion averaging. Broad direction/symmetry plausibility is required. When both raw controller and visible saber/grip transforms are available, later poses compare their relative controller-to-grip position and rotation with prior observations; a material change in that invariant relationship is rejected for retry. Natural wrist articulation between different arm poses is not treated as an outlier.

Starting Basic or Advanced calibration opens a dedicated 126-by-96 world-space wizard approximately 1.4 meters in front of the HMD. Its 0.011 world scale presents the expanded canvas at a readable physical size rather than BSML's oversized default. The wizard uses a BSML `FloatingScreen` with its real `VRGraphicRaycaster`, a hidden native Top handle, and custom background/border artwork with raycasts disabled. The handle covers the panel's reading surface but deliberately stops above the bottom control band: extending its physics collider through the controls causes a button click to become a panel grab before Unity's UI raycast can receive it. The result remains freely movable from the broad text/background area without a special visible grab strip, while every phase-specific button remains an ordinary interactive BSML control.

Persistent native BSML text objects follow the performance panel's proven ownership and ordinary `set_text` update model. One fixed-size root `VerticalLayoutGroup` is the only manually sized content rectangle; its content fitter is neutralized and native `LayoutElement` heights govern the text and action rows. The root transform is explicitly normalized, and a one-time post-layout geometry audit records the live screen, root, and text rectangles so a Quest-only collapse can be diagnosed from the support log rather than inferred from construction-time values. Curvature stays at the `FloatingScreen` constructor's zero value. Color and persistent section labels distinguish `CURRENT STEP`, `WHAT TO DO`, and `STATUS`, so only the values beneath those labels change as the sequence advances. The panel uses the HMD's horizontal facing direction only when it is initially placed, then remains fixed in world space until the player moves it. Looking around, crouching, or turning the body therefore cannot drag the UI through the user's view. `Recenter Panel` explicitly places the upright panel back at eye level in front of the user's current horizontal facing direction. A failed capture exposes Retry and Cancel on the panel. The completed capture set exposes Cancel, Restart, and Complete plus an overall/grip/reach/movement summary and low-confidence warnings.

The recenter path keeps the floating canvas at the viewer's yaw. Adding 180 degrees exposes the back of the canvas: UI panel images remain visible, while TextMeshPro glyphs are back-face culled. Calibration tick and shutter cues use the same ordinary `AudioSource` clip/play path as the measurement tone because Quest's `PlayOneShot` helper proved unsafe for this runtime-owned source.

The calibration wizard is ordinary interactive Unity UI and is intentionally not registered with the preview-surface exclusion path. Like Beat Saber's other menus, it remains visible to the third-person camera, previews, and recordings. The working docked/floor preview, the movable popout preview, and the compact recording-control surface all remain excluded from spectator rendering; they are camera monitors and HMD-only controls, not scene content.

Static instructions describe the pose to make during the countdown and hold through the tone. Motion instructions explicitly require the natural ready pose to be held until the tone begins. Neck-only look steps keep controllers still in that ready pose and keep shoulders, waist, and feet facing forward; whole-body turn steps instead rotate feet, hips, waist, shoulders, and head together and hold the final angle through the shutter. Lean/squat/duck steps return before the shutter, while translational steps remain at the new position through the shutter.

## Avatar menu workflow

Changing avatars is a three-step operation: choose the `.vrm` file, select **Load Avatar**, then select **Attach Tracking**. A successfully loaded avatar safely replaces the previous avatar, so using **Unload Avatar** first is optional. Loading without tracking intentionally leaves the model in its rest pose for visual inspection; attaching tracking connects it to the HMD/controller solver.

**Resync Player Pose** refreshes the transient standing height, floor, tracking origin, headset, and controller reference used by the currently loaded avatar. It is useful after recentering, changing play height, or noticing that the avatar is vertically misaligned. It does not delete the saved Basic or Advanced calibration profile. **Reset Saved Profile** is the destructive operation: it deletes those saved measurements and returns the solver to generic player defaults.

Motion validation distinguishes return-to-origin lean from persistent translation using requested direction, peak and final normalized displacement, controller-midpoint evidence, duration, velocity, head tilt, and return fraction. Squat, duck, look, and turn captures have separate transparent checks. Failed data never advances silently. Retry replaces only the current step; Cancel restores the previously active profile; Reset removes the saved profile and returns to generic defaults.

Finishing the last accepted capture fits a pending profile and enters Review. It does not serialize the pending result and does not replace the gameplay `RuntimePlayerProfile`. Cancel discards it, Restart reruns the same Basic or Advanced plan, and only Complete atomically saves and activates it. If saving fails, the fitted result stays pending so Retry can attempt the save again while the previous profile remains active.

## Grip and reach fitting

The fitter constructs one per-side source-to-canonical-hand rotation across all accepted arm poses and reports mean/maximum rotational residual. When four or more visible saber-grip observations exist, that source is preferred and one controller-to-visible-grip pose is fitted with mean/maximum positional residual. Otherwise the fit explicitly records that it is controller-derived and does not invent a visible-grip transform. A later per-profile/avatar manual grip adjustment is one rigid local target transform layered after this fit. Its player-oriented axis arrows and fine sliders edit that same transform: hold the trigger while dragging an arrow to translate along its axis, or hold the side grip while dragging to rotate around that axis. Finger closure and thumb opposition are independent because they wrap opposite sides of a round controller grip. Position, rotation, finger closure, and thumb curve are stored independently for each hand in the selected avatar's profile; `Mirror To Other + Save` reflects the rigid transform to the opposite hand and copies the finger settings. The temporary `Show Avatar Arm` view is not persisted. Calibration data remains unchanged, and Cancel is non-destructive.

At runtime a controller-derived anatomical correction is converted through the simultaneously sampled controller-to-saber rotation before it is applied to an active saber handle. Per-side grip and reach fits also have explicit minimum confidence gates. In menus, a measured controller-to-grip pose is used only when it has enough observations and confidence; otherwise the existing explicit controller-to-wrist fallback remains authoritative. Low-confidence reach data similarly leaves measured avatar arm length unchanged. This prevents a weak or menu-only fit from applying controller axes directly to a differently oriented gameplay saber.

Effective reach is stored independently for each side and includes the 85th-percentile effective reach, maximum comfortable sample, and labeled down/forward/overhead/cross-body regions. It changes bounded reach-space/shoulder behavior without changing whole-avatar scale. Controller-only targets retain the five-percent visual stretch cap. An active tracked saber handle is instead a hard hand target: the arm may use up to ten-percent emergency stretch for proportion mismatch, but any remaining anatomical reach error is recorded rather than allowing the visible hand to leave the saber. Finger curls are derived from the loaded avatar's own rest-pose geometry and applied around that live handle; they are not part of the saved player profile.

## Runtime motion model

Four directional lean radii form a quadrant-aware ellipse, so diagonal motion has no hard left/right/forward sector jump. Lean and step signatures retain displacement, controller midpoint, velocity, head tilt, duration, return/persistence, and confidence. The deterministic runtime scorer produces lean and translation confidence, envelope utilization, and four step similarities using only fixed values. Lean keeps the pelvis/support comparatively stable and uses the existing continuous torso chain; step-like persistent HMD-plus-controller motion begins pelvis translation and step preparation earlier. Anatomical support, spine, leg-reach, and one-foot-at-a-time constraints remain authoritative.

Squat/forward-duck samples tune the existing geometric vertical-drop/forward-hinge mapping. Left/right turn samples tune the existing `LOCKED`/`TURNING`/`SETTLING` soft cone, dwell, settle time, and yaw rate rather than replacing that state machine.

## Performance and diagnostics

Calibration capture and fitting may allocate, sort, and serialize because they run deliberately outside normal play. `RuntimePlayerProfile`, the classifier state, and solver diagnostics contain no dynamic containers. The profile-aware gameplay solve performs no heap allocation, trajectory search, fitting, JSON work, reflection, or ML inference.

`Write Diagnostic Log` reports profile validity/confidence, last capture confidence, current classification, lean/translation confidence, ellipse utilization, four step similarities, turn confidence, per-hand effective reach ratio and grip residual, plus pre-anchor versus final hand error and whether the saber grip was hard-anchored. Existing body, spine, foot, timing, and reach diagnostics remain. It is still an explicit user action rather than a per-frame log.

## Validation status and stop point

Synthetic host coverage verifies introduction idling, automatic versus step-by-step progression gates, fixed multi-pose grip recovery (including quaternion sign equivalence), independent hands, unstable-sample rejection, outlier retry, five-second cue sequencing, detailed validation output, Basic-only confidence accounting, controller-only versus visible-grip provenance, serialization/refit, version/incomplete/malformed/missing fallback, pending review/cancel/explicit-completion semantics, return lean, persistent slow/fast translation, diagonal lean, head-only tilt, controller-only arm movement, squat/duck classification, turn/glance behavior, and zero per-frame allocations with an active profile. The ARM64 Quest library also compiles.

The first on-headset attempts exposed ambiguous motion timing and profile-completion failures. The corrected workflow now emits per-step measurements and rejection reasons to the SaberStage log so the next authorized Quest pass can identify any remaining device-specific threshold problem. Personalized calibration cannot be claimed visually better than the generic solver before a complete same-player Black Heart calibration and before/after recording pass.
